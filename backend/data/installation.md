# Solar Installation, Maintenance and Warranty

## Customer Assessment
Collect city, monthly consumption, recent bills, major appliances, peak/critical load, backup requirement, roof area, shading, grid availability, system type and budget.

## Site Survey
Inspect roof condition and structure, dimensions, shading, orientation/tilt, cable routes, distribution board, inverter/battery locations, earthing and safe access.

## Installation Process
1. Site survey
2. Load assessment
3. System design
4. Mounting structure
5. Panel installation
6. DC wiring
7. Inverter installation
8. Battery installation if required
9. AC connection/protection
10. Earthing
11. Testing
12. Commissioning

## Protection
Depending on design: DC/AC isolators, breakers/fuses, surge protection, earthing, correctly sized cables and battery protection.

## Testing
Verify polarity, string voltage/current, insulation, earthing, inverter settings, grid parameters, battery communication, protection, monitoring and fault history.

## Maintenance
Inspect panels for dust, cracks, hot spots, loose mounting and damaged cables. Check inverter alarms, ventilation, monitoring and fault history. Check batteries for state of charge, temperature, alarms, communication and physical condition.

## Cleaning
Frequency depends on dust, rainfall, bird activity and site conditions. The project research suggests 6–12 months as general guidance, but dusty sites may need more frequent cleaning. Follow manufacturer instructions.

## Warranty
Product, performance, inverter and battery warranties are model-specific. Battery warranties may depend on years, cycles, throughput, DoD and operating conditions. Never promise warranty coverage without checking the actual warranty document.

## Grid Connection
Grid-connected systems may require utility approval/interconnection and metering procedures. Requirements can differ by utility and change over time.

## Recommendation Examples
**Small home:** a 3 kW system can be an initial comparison for modest loads. Final sizing needs bills, loads, roof area and shading.

**Medium home:** a 5 kW system can be an initial comparison. If backup is required, compare hybrid systems and battery capacity.

**Large home/business:** 10–20 kW systems require careful phase selection, string design, roof assessment, load profile and utility requirements.

All examples are **initial estimates only** and are not final engineering designs.
